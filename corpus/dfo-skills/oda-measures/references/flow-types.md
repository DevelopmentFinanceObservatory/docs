# Flow types

What this covers: the four ODA flow types reported by the OECD DAC, which questions each fits, ONE's usual choices, and the 2018 methodology break.

## When to use

This reference informs questions about which ODA measure to use, what a specific figure such as "grant equivalents" means, how to compare ODA across donors or years, and why two ODA figures for the same donor or year differ.

## Concepts and definitions

Disbursements are actual payments made in a given year. They are the measure of ODA flows, and reveal donor intentions and policy implementation.

Gross disbursements are the total a donor disburses in a year, before subtracting loan repayments received from borrowers. Grant-equivalent data is not always available at activity level, so gross disbursements are the measure for breakdowns by sector, channel, instrument, or modality.

Net disbursements are gross disbursements minus loan repayments received in the same year. They show net resource transfers to a recipient, the amount a donor paid out beyond what it received back. They are negative when repayments in a year exceed new disbursements. For example, if Japan disbursed US$5 billion in loans and received US$3 billion in repayments, its net disbursements are US$2 billion. They suit specific questions about resource transfers and are less common in detailed analyses.

Grant equivalents count only the concessional portion of a loan as ODA. A loan's grant equivalent is its disbursement multiplied by its grant element, the share of the loan that is a financial "gift" relative to a market loan. Grants count in full because their grant element is 100%. The grant element is calculated at commitment using income-differentiated discount rates and is not recalculated at disbursement. The DAC adopted the grant-equivalent methodology in 2014 to better reflect donor effort and to encourage more concessional lending to low-income countries. Grant equivalents are the OECD's headline measure from 2018 data. Before 2018 the headline and the ODA/GNI ratio use net flows.

Commitments are the amount a donor formally agrees to provide, regardless of when it is paid. They measure donor intentions and allow monitoring of resource targeting. A commitment may be disbursed over several years, so commitments are a poor proxy for actual flows. For example, a donor that commits US$200 million to a five-year infrastructure programme in 2022 records the commitment in 2022 and records disbursements each year as payments are made.

## The 2018 methodology break

Headline ODA uses grant equivalents from 2018 data, first reported in 2019, and earlier headline figures use the cash-flow methodology, which counts net disbursements, so the two are not directly comparable. The OECD also publishes grant-equivalent figures for 2015–2017 and a parallel cash-flow series that is comparable across the full period. An analysis that spans 2018 can therefore use the cash-flow series throughout, stay on one side of the break, or span the break with a caveat.

## Choosing a flow type

Grant equivalents suit questions about aggregate donor effort and ODA/GNI, because they reflect the true cost to the donor of concessional loans. Net disbursements suit questions about what a recipient received. Gross disbursements suit questions about where ODA went, by sector, channel, instrument, or modality. A question that combines a headline figure with a granular breakdown can need a different measure for each part. Commitments suit questions about what donors intend to provide.

When the user names no basis, ONE chooses the measure from the shape of the question and uses disbursements rather than commitments for actual flows:

| Question | Years | Measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Donor only, no recipient or sector | 2018 or later | Grant equivalents (OECD headline) |
| Donor only | Before 2018 | Net disbursements (OECD headline before 2018) |
| Donor only | Crossing 2018 | OECD headline for each year: net disbursements to 2017, grant equivalents from 2018, shown as two periods with the break marked |
| Recipient, all sectors, from any donors or from a named donor | Any | Net disbursements (DAC2A receipts) |
| Any sector, channel, type of finance (instrument), or cooperation modality | Any | Gross disbursements (CRS) |

A user's stated basis ("net", "gross", "grant equivalent", "commitments") overrides the table. A stated price basis (current or constant prices) applies to the table's measure. A basis or price basis the user chose earlier in the conversation carries over to later questions, and the answer states the carried choice. When the later question's data holds no series counted on the carried basis, the answer uses the basis the table gives for that question's shape. The two periods of a series that crosses 2018 hold different years and do not add, and a growth figure across 2017 to 2018 carries the break marker.

A total for one named donor read from the CRS is the donor's bilateral ODA plus its imputed multilateral ODA, in gross disbursements at current prices, when ONE's imputed multilateral sectors dataset covers every year asked for (it starts in 2011). That dataset breaks a donor's imputed ODA down by recipient and sector only. A total restricted to a type of finance, a cooperation modality, or a channel counts bilateral flows, and so does a total at constant prices, on another basis, or over years the dataset does not cover.

For a window that starts before 2011, the bilateral-plus-imputed sum is offered over the years the dataset covers. It is not offered at constant prices, on a basis other than gross disbursements, or for a total restricted to a type of finance, a cooperation modality, or a channel.

A named donor's total to a named recipient, with no sector, is DAC2A total ODA in net disbursements, which counts bilateral flows only. The bilateral-plus-imputed sum applies only when the user asks for the donor's contribution through multilateral channels as well.

DAC1 total ODA already includes the donor's core multilateral contributions, so it takes no imputed addition.

## Pitfalls

- Grant equivalents and gross disbursements in the same chart or table, without labels, lead readers to draw incorrect conclusions.
- Net disbursements used without explanation confuse readers who expect gross figures.
- Grant equivalents are a measurement concept applied to all ODA, including loans, and differ from grants, which are non-repayable transfers. A donor with a large loan portfolio has grant equivalents well below its gross disbursements.
- Two figures for the same donor that look different often reflect different flow types or different series (cash-flow and grant-equivalent) rather than a data error. The series a data source uses is not always obvious.

## What makes a result verifiable

A result is verifiable when it states its flow type and the reason for it, states which series the data uses, and flags the 2018 break when the analysis spans it. The result should state the flow type for each part of an analysis that uses a different measure.
